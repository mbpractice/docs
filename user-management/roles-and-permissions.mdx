Your EHR uses a role-based access control (RBAC) model to ensure that every staff member can access exactly what they need to do their job — and nothing more. When you assign a role to a user, they automatically inherit a predefined set of permissions aligned to their clinical or administrative function. This approach reduces configuration overhead, supports least-privilege security principles, and helps your practice stay audit-ready.

## Standard Roles

The following roles are available out of the box. Each role is designed to match a real-world function within a healthcare practice.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Administrator" icon="shield-halved">
    Full access to all system areas, including user management, settings, billing configuration, and clinical data. Typically assigned to practice owners or office managers.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Provider (Physician / NP / PA)" icon="user-doctor">
    Full access to patient records, clinical notes, e-prescribing, and lab/imaging orders for patients under their care. Can view but not modify billing configuration.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Clinical Staff (MA / RN)" icon="stethoscope">
    Can view and update patient records, document vitals and intake notes, and assist with orders. Cannot prescribe independently or access billing data.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Front Desk" icon="calendar">
    Focused on scheduling and patient demographics. Can create and manage appointments, check patients in, and update contact information. No access to clinical notes or prescriptions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Biller" icon="file-invoice-dollar">
    Access to insurance claims, billing records, payment posting, and coding tools. No access to clinical notes beyond what is required for coding. Cannot modify user accounts or settings.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Roles and Permissions Matrix

The table below summarizes what each standard role can do across the major functional areas of the platform. **Full** means full read/write access, **View** means read-only, and **—** means no access.

| Permission Area     | Administrator | Provider | Clinical Staff | Front Desk | Biller |
|---------------------|:-------------:|:--------:|:--------------:|:----------:|:------:|
| Patient Records     | Full          | Full     | Full           | View       | View   |
| Scheduling          | Full          | Full     | Full           | Full       | —      |
| Clinical Notes      | Full          | Full     | View           | —          | View   |
| Prescriptions       | Full          | Full     | —              | —          | —      |
| Billing & Claims    | Full          | View     | —              | —          | Full   |
| User Management     | Full          | —        | —              | —          | —      |
| Practice Settings   | Full          | —        | —              | —          | —      |

<Note>
  Under HIPAA's minimum necessary standard, users should only be granted access to the protected health information (PHI) required to perform their specific job duties. Review role assignments regularly — especially after staff changes — to ensure your access controls remain compliant. Your EHR audit log records every access and modification to patient records.
</Note>

## Custom Roles and Per-User Permissions

If the standard roles don't perfectly match your practice's structure, you can create custom roles or adjust permissions at the individual user level.

### Creating a Custom Role

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Role Management">
    Navigate to **Settings → Users & Roles → Roles** and click **New Role**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name and describe the role">
    Give the role a clear, descriptive name (for example, "Triage Nurse" or "Billing Supervisor") and an optional description so other administrators understand its purpose.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set permissions">
    Toggle individual permissions on or off using the permission builder. Permissions are grouped by module: Patient Records, Scheduling, Clinical, Prescriptions, Billing, Administration.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and assign">
    Click **Save Role**, then assign it to users from **Settings → Users** just as you would a standard role.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Adjusting Per-User Permissions

For one-off situations, you can override a specific permission for an individual user without changing their role.

1. Go to **Settings → Users** and select the user.
2. Click the **Permissions** tab on their profile.
3. Toggle the specific permission you want to add or remove. Overrides are shown with a badge so you can distinguish them from role defaults.
4. Click **Save Changes**.

<Tip>
  Use per-user overrides sparingly. Managing access through roles is far easier to audit and maintain over time. If you find yourself frequently overriding the same permission, consider creating a dedicated custom role instead.
</Tip>

## Reviewing and Auditing Access

You can review who has access to what at any time from **Settings → Users & Roles → Access Report**. This report shows every user, their assigned role, any permission overrides, and the date their access was last modified — making it straightforward to prepare for HIPAA risk assessments or internal audits.
