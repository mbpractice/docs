Your EHR connects to a growing catalog of third-party applications that extend its capabilities without requiring you to leave the platform or manually transfer data. Whether you are routing claims through a clearinghouse, launching a virtual visit, sending automated appointment reminders, or pulling in blood pressure readings from a patient's home device, each integration is set up through the **Integration Marketplace** and managed centrally from **Settings → Integrations**. Once connected, data flows automatically between systems so your team can focus on care rather than administration.

## Billing Clearinghouses

Clearinghouse integrations automate the full claim lifecycle. Once connected, charges generated from a finalized encounter are transmitted electronically to the clearinghouse, which scrubs the claim, routes it to the correct payer, and returns the remittance advice for automated payment posting. You can check real-time eligibility before appointments and receive prior authorization responses without leaving the EHR.

To configure a clearinghouse, go to **Settings → Integrations → Billing Clearinghouses**, select your clearinghouse partner, and enter the submitter ID and enrollment credentials provided during your clearinghouse enrollment. Payer routing tables sync automatically once the connection is active.

## Telehealth Platforms

Telehealth integrations embed the virtual visit workflow directly into your scheduling and encounter experience. When you schedule a telehealth appointment, the system generates a unique visit link and sends it to the patient via automated reminder. Providers join from the appointment scheduler or from a direct link in the encounter note. Post-visit documentation proceeds identically to an in-person encounter.

To activate a telehealth integration, go to **Settings → Integrations → Telehealth**, select your platform, and complete the OAuth authorization flow using your telehealth provider account credentials.

## Patient Engagement Platforms

Patient engagement integrations handle the communication touchpoints that happen outside the clinical visit: appointment reminders, recall campaigns, post-visit surveys, secure messaging, and digital intake forms. Data collected through these tools — completed intake forms, consent signatures, and patient-reported information — flows back into the chart automatically so staff do not need to re-enter it.

## Practice Management Systems

If your organization uses a separate practice management system (PMS) for scheduling or billing, a bidirectional sync integration keeps patient demographics, appointments, and charges in sync between both systems. Changes made in either system are reflected in the other within minutes, eliminating the duplicate entry that typically causes reconciliation errors at month-end.

## Remote Patient Monitoring

Remote patient monitoring (RPM) integrations pull readings from connected patient devices — blood pressure cuffs, continuous glucose monitors, pulse oximeters, and scales — into a dedicated **RPM Dashboard** in the patient chart. Alert thresholds can be configured per patient so that readings outside the target range trigger a care team notification. Time spent reviewing RPM data is logged automatically to support RPM billing under CPT codes 99457 and 99458.

## Connect a New Third-Party App

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Integration Marketplace">
    Go to **Settings → Integrations → Marketplace**. Use the category filters or search bar to find the application you want to connect.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the Integration Details">
    Click the application tile to open its detail page. Review what data the integration accesses, what permissions it requests, and whether a BAA is required. Download the BAA template from this page if one is provided.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Authorize the Connection">
    Click **Connect**. Depending on the integration type, you will either:
    - Be redirected to the third-party application to complete an **OAuth authorization flow**, or
    - Be prompted to enter an **API key or account credentials** provided by the third-party vendor.

    Follow the on-screen prompts to grant the requested permissions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure Integration Settings">
    After authorization, configure the integration-specific settings that appear on the connection page. This may include mapping provider accounts, setting sync frequency, selecting which locations are included, or configuring data filters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload the Signed BAA">
    If the integration involves PHI, upload the signed Business Associate Agreement to **Settings → Integrations → [App Name] → Compliance Documents** before enabling data sync. The integration will remain in **Pending BAA** status until a valid BAA document is on file.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify the Connection">
    Click **Test Connection** to confirm that data is flowing correctly. Review the **Sync Log** tab to inspect the first data exchange and confirm that records are mapping to the correct patients and providers.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Managing OAuth Permissions and Revoking Access

All OAuth-based integrations are listed under **Settings → Integrations → Connected Apps**. For each connected application, you can:

- **View granted permissions** — see exactly which data scopes the application has access to (e.g., read patient demographics, write encounter notes, read/write appointments).
- **Review last activity** — see when the application last made an API call, which helps identify unused or dormant connections.
- **Rotate credentials** — generate a new OAuth token or API key without disconnecting the integration, useful when rotating credentials as part of a security review.
- **Revoke access** — immediately invalidate all tokens and credentials for the application, cutting off its access to your EHR data within seconds.

Revocation takes effect instantly. The disconnected application will receive an authorization error on its next API call and will need to go through the authorization flow again to reconnect. All revocation events are logged in the compliance audit trail with the timestamp and the administrator who performed the action.

<Warning>
  Before activating any third-party integration that will receive, transmit, or store **protected health information (PHI)**, confirm that a signed **Business Associate Agreement (BAA)** is in place with that vendor. Using a third-party service to handle PHI without a valid BAA is a violation of HIPAA's Privacy and Security Rules and can result in significant fines for your organization. Do not rely on a vendor's general terms of service as a substitute for a BAA. If a vendor is unwilling to sign a BAA, do not connect the integration. Contact your compliance officer if you have questions about whether a specific integration involves PHI.
</Warning>
