Your EHR platform is built from the ground up with HIPAA compliance as a foundational requirement, not an afterthought. Every feature that touches protected health information (PHI) — from patient record storage and clinical notes to e-prescribing and lab results — is designed to meet or exceed the technical, administrative, and physical safeguards required by the HIPAA Security Rule. This page explains each layer of protection and what you need to know to operate your practice in a compliant manner.

<Note>
  Your EHR platform provides the technical infrastructure for HIPAA compliance, but **you and your organization remain responsible for your own compliance obligations**. This includes training your workforce, establishing internal policies and procedures, and ensuring your use of the platform aligns with HIPAA requirements. If you have specific legal questions, consult a qualified healthcare attorney or compliance officer.
</Note>

## Business Associate Agreement (BAA)

A Business Associate Agreement is a legally required contract between a covered entity (your practice) and any vendor that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on your behalf. Your EHR vendor qualifies as a business associate, which means a signed BAA must be in place before you use the platform to store or process patient data.

**How to request and sign your BAA:**

1. Navigate to **Settings → Organization → Compliance** in your dashboard.
2. Click **Request Business Associate Agreement**.
3. Review the agreement with your legal or compliance team.
4. Sign electronically using the built-in e-signature workflow.
5. Download and retain a copy for your records.

Your executed BAA is permanently stored in the Compliance section of your account and can be re-downloaded at any time. If your organization structure changes — for example, you add a new legal entity or acquire another practice — contact support to update your BAA accordingly.

## Data Encryption

All PHI stored and transmitted through your EHR platform is protected by industry-standard encryption at every stage of its lifecycle.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Encryption at Rest" icon="hard-drive">
    All data stored on the platform — including patient records, clinical notes, lab results, and attachments — is encrypted using **AES-256**, the same standard used by financial institutions and government agencies. Database volumes, backups, and file storage are all encrypted at the infrastructure level.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Encryption in Transit" icon="shield-halved">
    All data transmitted between your browser, mobile devices, and the platform uses **TLS 1.2 or higher**. Connections that attempt to use older, less secure protocols are rejected automatically. API integrations are also required to communicate over TLS.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Key Management" icon="key">
    Encryption keys are managed using a dedicated key management service (KMS) with strict access controls and automated key rotation. No single person or process can access both the encrypted data and the keys simultaneously.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Backup Encryption" icon="database">
    Automated daily backups are encrypted using the same AES-256 standard before being written to geographically redundant storage. Backup integrity is verified on a regular schedule.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Access Controls

Limiting who can access PHI is one of the most important safeguards in any HIPAA compliance program. Your EHR platform provides layered access controls that let you enforce the principle of minimum necessary access.

### Role-Based Permissions

Every user in your organization is assigned a role that determines which parts of the system they can access. Built-in roles include Physician, Nurse, Medical Assistant, Biller, Receptionist, and Administrator. You can also create custom roles with granular permission sets tailored to your workflows.

To manage roles, go to **Settings → Users & Roles → Role Management**.

### Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA is **strongly recommended** for all users and **required** for any user with administrative privileges. When MFA is enabled, users must provide a second factor — such as a time-based one-time password (TOTP) from an authenticator app — in addition to their password at each login.

To enforce MFA organization-wide, navigate to **Settings → Security → Authentication** and toggle **Require MFA for all users**.

### Session Timeouts

To reduce the risk of unauthorized access from unattended workstations — a common issue in clinical environments — the platform automatically logs out inactive sessions. The default timeout is **15 minutes**, which meets common HIPAA guidance for workstation security. You can adjust the timeout period (up to a maximum of 60 minutes) under **Settings → Security → Session Management**.

<Tip>
  Conduct a **quarterly access review** to audit which users have access to which parts of the system. Terminate access for former employees promptly and downgrade permissions for users whose roles have changed. You can generate a full user access report from **Settings → Users & Roles → Access Report**.
</Tip>

## Audit Logging

Every interaction with PHI in your EHR platform generates an immutable audit log entry. Audit logs capture who accessed or modified what data, when the action occurred, and from which IP address or device. This satisfies the HIPAA requirement for audit controls and provides an evidentiary trail for breach investigations, compliance audits, and internal reviews.

Logged events include:
- Patient record views, edits, and deletions
- Clinical note creation and amendments
- Prescription transmissions
- Lab and imaging order placements
- Document exports and downloads
- User logins, logouts, and failed login attempts
- Administrative changes to users and roles

Audit logs are retained for **7 years** in accordance with HIPAA medical record retention guidelines and cannot be edited or deleted by any user, including administrators. For full details on accessing and interpreting your audit logs, see the [Audit Logs](/compliance/audit-logs) page.

## Data Retention and Deletion

### Retention Policy

Patient records and associated clinical data are retained for a minimum of **7 years** from the date of last activity, or longer if required by your state's medical record retention laws. Some states require longer retention periods for pediatric records (until the patient reaches adulthood plus a defined number of years). You are responsible for knowing and applying the retention rules for your jurisdiction.

### Data Deletion

You can delete individual patient records or request a full account data purge. Deletion requests are processed as follows:

- **Soft delete:** The record is immediately hidden from all users and workflows but remains in encrypted storage during a **30-day recovery window**.
- **Hard delete:** After 30 days, or upon explicit confirmation, data is permanently deleted from all active systems and removed from backups on the next backup rotation cycle.

<Warning>
  Data deletion is **irreversible** once the 30-day recovery window has passed. Before deleting any patient record, verify that you have met all applicable retention requirements and obtained any necessary authorizations. Contact support immediately if you need to recover a recently deleted record.
</Warning>

## Breach Notification Procedures

In the event of a security incident or suspected breach involving PHI, your EHR platform follows a defined breach notification process aligned with the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule.

**Platform responsibilities:**
- The security team will investigate and contain the incident within **24 hours** of detection.
- You will be notified of a confirmed breach affecting your organization's data **within 48 hours** via your registered account email and a direct call to your organization's primary contact.
- A written incident report detailing the nature of the breach, data involved, timeline, and remediation steps will be provided **within 10 business days**.

**Your responsibilities:**
- Upon notification, you must assess whether the breach triggers your own notification obligations under HIPAA (notifying affected patients within 60 days, notifying HHS, and in some cases notifying media outlets).
- Document your breach response activities in your own incident response log.
- Work with your legal counsel to determine if state breach notification laws impose additional or shorter deadlines.

To report a suspected security incident on your end, contact **security@ehrplatform.com** or call the dedicated security hotline listed in your BAA and the Compliance section of your dashboard.
