Electronic prescribing eliminates paper prescriptions, reduces transcription errors, and sends medication orders directly to the patient's preferred pharmacy in seconds. The EHR's integrated e-prescribing module connects to the Surescripts network, giving you real-time access to pharmacy routing, formulary data, and drug interaction checking — all without leaving the patient's chart.

## Prescribing a New Medication

Send a new prescription to the patient's preferred pharmacy directly from an encounter note or from the patient's medication list.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the prescription writer">
    From within a signed or open encounter note, click **+ New Prescription** in the Plan section. Alternatively, navigate to the patient's chart, go to the **Medications** tab, and click **+ Prescribe**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Search for the medication">
    Begin typing the drug name, brand name, or active ingredient in the **Medication Search** field. The system searches the full drug database and returns matching results with available formulations.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the drug, strength, and formulation">
    Choose the correct drug from the search results. Select the appropriate **strength** and **formulation** (tablet, capsule, liquid, patch, inhaler, etc.) from the dropdown menus.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter dosing instructions">
    Complete the **Sig** field with dosing instructions: dose, route, frequency, and any special instructions (for example, "Take with food" or "Use as directed"). Use the Sig builder to construct structured instructions, or type free text.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set quantity and refills">
    Enter the **Quantity** to dispense and the number of **Refills** authorized. Review the formulary status indicator (see [Formulary Checker](#formulary-checker) below) before finalizing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review drug interaction alerts">
    The system automatically checks the new medication against the patient's current medications, allergies, and documented diagnoses. Review any alerts that appear before proceeding (see [Drug Interaction Alerts](#drug-interaction-alerts)).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the pharmacy">
    Confirm the **Preferred Pharmacy** shown, or search for a different pharmacy by name, zip code, or chain. The patient's preferred pharmacy is pre-selected if it has been saved to their profile.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the prescription">
    Click **Send Prescription**. The order is transmitted electronically to the pharmacy via the Surescripts network. A transmission confirmation appears within a few seconds.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Add frequently prescribed medications to your **Favorites** list by clicking the star icon next to a drug in the search results. Favorited medications appear at the top of future searches so you can prescribe your most common medications in a fraction of the time.
</Tip>

## Prescription Renewal Requests

When a patient requests a refill, their pharmacy routes the renewal request electronically to your inbox. You can approve, modify, or deny the request without a patient visit.

**To manage renewal requests:**

1. Navigate to **Inbox → Renewal Requests**. Pending requests are listed with the drug name, requesting pharmacy, and patient name.
2. Click a request to open the renewal detail view. Review the patient's current medication list, last dispense date, and any attached notes from the pharmacy.
3. Select one of the following actions:
   - **Approve** — sends the renewal as requested.
   - **Approve with Changes** — opens the prescription editor so you can modify dose, quantity, or refills before sending.
   - **Deny** — rejects the request and sends a denial notice to the pharmacy. Enter a reason (required).
4. Click **Confirm** to transmit your response to the pharmacy.

<Info>
  Renewal requests older than 72 hours are flagged in your inbox with a yellow indicator. Requests older than 7 days are escalated to your practice manager. Configure escalation thresholds in **Settings → E-Prescribing → Renewal Routing**.
</Info>

## EPCS — Electronic Prescribing for Controlled Substances

EPCS (Electronic Prescribing for Controlled Substances) allows you to prescribe Schedule II–V medications electronically, eliminating paper prescriptions and improving auditability. EPCS is subject to strict DEA and state-level regulations and requires a one-time identity verification and two-factor authentication setup before use.

<Warning>
  **EPCS requires identity proofing before activation.** The DEA mandates that every prescriber who uses EPCS complete identity proofing at the **NIST IAL2** level. You must verify your identity through the built-in identity proofing workflow before you can send your first controlled substance prescription electronically. This is a one-time process per prescriber, but your credentials must be re-verified if your DEA registration lapses or if you change organizations. Contact your practice administrator if you need to begin the identity proofing process.
</Warning>

### Setting up EPCS

<Steps>
  <Step title="Begin identity proofing">
    Navigate to **Settings → My Profile → EPCS Setup** and click **Start Identity Proofing**. You will be guided through a DEA-compliant identity verification workflow that includes government-issued ID verification and a knowledge-based authentication (KBA) quiz.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set up two-factor authentication">
    After identity proofing, enroll in two-factor authentication using an **authenticator app** (recommended) or a **hardware token**. EPCS requires two-factor authentication for every controlled substance prescription you sign — this is a DEA requirement, not an optional setting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your DEA registration">
    Enter your DEA registration number and expiration date. The system validates your DEA number against the DEA Controlled Substance Act registrant database. Your DEA number must be current and active.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Acknowledge the EPCS policy">
    Review and acknowledge your organization's EPCS policy. Your signature is recorded and retained for audit purposes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Sending a controlled substance prescription

The process for prescribing a controlled substance is identical to prescribing a non-controlled medication (see [Prescribing a New Medication](#prescribing-a-new-medication)) with one additional step:

- After clicking **Send Prescription**, a two-factor authentication prompt appears. Enter your authenticator app code or hardware token code to confirm your identity and authorize the transmission.

<Note>
  Controlled substance prescribing rules vary significantly by state. Some states restrict the number of days' supply that can be prescribed at one time, prohibit certain Schedule II medications from being prescribed via EPCS (a small number of exceptions still exist), or require additional patient-prescriber relationship documentation. Always verify your state's current regulations before prescribing controlled substances. The EHR displays state-specific alerts based on the patient's state of residence and your practice location, but these alerts supplement — they do not replace — your professional and legal obligations.
</Note>

## Drug Interaction Alerts

The EHR automatically checks every new prescription against the patient's complete medication list, documented allergies, and known drug-disease interactions. Alerts appear inline in the prescription writer before you transmit.

### Alert severity levels

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Contraindicated" icon="circle-xmark">
    **Do not prescribe.** The combination poses a serious risk of patient harm. The prescription cannot be sent until you document a clinical override with a justification. Override decisions are logged for audit review.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Severe Interaction" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    **Use with caution.** The interaction may cause significant clinical effects. You can proceed after acknowledging the alert and documenting your clinical rationale.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Moderate / Informational" icon="circle-info">
    **Be aware.** The interaction is clinically relevant but manageable. These alerts are informational — you can dismiss them with a single click after reviewing.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**To manage an interaction alert:**

1. Read the full alert text, which includes the mechanism of interaction and recommended clinical guidance.
2. For **Contraindicated** alerts: document your clinical override justification in the text field and select a reason from the dropdown before proceeding.
3. For **Severe** alerts: click **Acknowledge and Proceed** after reviewing the alert.
4. For **Moderate / Informational** alerts: click **Dismiss** to continue.

<Tip>
  All interaction alert overrides are recorded in the patient's chart under **Medication History → Override Log**. Your compliance officer can review this log during quality audits.
</Tip>

## Formulary Checker

The formulary checker tells you in real time whether a medication is covered under the patient's insurance plan and at what cost tier — before you send the prescription.

**Formulary status indicators appear next to every drug in the search results:**

| Indicator | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **Preferred** | Covered at the lowest cost tier — typically a generic or preferred brand |
| 🟡 **Non-Preferred** | Covered but at a higher cost tier — a therapeutic alternative may reduce patient cost |
| 🔴 **Non-Covered** | Not covered under the patient's current plan — prior authorization or alternative required |
| ⚪ **No Data** | Formulary data is not available for this patient's plan |

When a medication is **Non-Preferred** or **Non-Covered**, click **Therapeutic Alternatives** to see covered drugs in the same therapeutic class, ranked by formulary tier and cost. Switching to a therapeutically equivalent preferred drug can significantly reduce out-of-pocket costs for your patients.

**Prior Authorization (PA):**
When a PA is required, a **PA Required** badge appears next to the drug. Click **Start Prior Authorization** to initiate a PA request electronically through the Surescripts CoverMyMeds integration. You can track PA status from **Inbox → Prior Authorizations**.

## Preferred Pharmacy Settings

Patients can designate one or more preferred pharmacies to receive their prescriptions. The preferred pharmacy is pre-selected every time you prescribe, so you don't have to search for it on each visit.

### Setting a patient's preferred pharmacy

1. Open the patient's chart and go to **Demographics → Preferred Pharmacy**.
2. Click **+ Add Pharmacy** and search by pharmacy name, address, or zip code.
3. Select the pharmacy from the results and click **Save**. The pharmacy is now saved as the patient's default for all future prescriptions.

Patients can also update their own preferred pharmacy through the **Patient Portal** under **Profile → Pharmacies**.

### Specialty and mail-order pharmacies

For specialty medications or chronic disease medications requiring mail-order dispensing:

1. Use the pharmacy search and filter by **Type → Specialty** or **Type → Mail Order**.
2. Select the appropriate specialty or mail-order pharmacy.
3. Note that some specialty medications require additional prior authorization documentation before the pharmacy will dispense — the EHR flags these automatically during the prescribing workflow.

<Info>
  If a patient's preferred pharmacy is unable to receive electronic prescriptions (for example, a compounding pharmacy that is not Surescripts-enabled), you can generate a **printed prescription** or **fax order** from the prescription writer by selecting the appropriate transmission method before clicking **Send**.
</Info>
