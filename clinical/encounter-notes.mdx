Accurate, timely encounter documentation is the foundation of quality patient care and compliant medical billing. The EHR provides a structured environment for creating, editing, signing, and co-signing clinical notes — all linked directly to the patient's appointment, problem list, and billing encounter so nothing falls through the cracks.

## Starting an Encounter from an Appointment

Launch a new encounter directly from the patient's scheduled appointment to ensure the note is automatically associated with the correct visit date, provider, and billing codes.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the appointment">
    Navigate to **Schedule** and locate the patient's appointment for today. Click the appointment block to open the appointment detail panel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start the encounter">
    Click **Start Encounter** in the appointment detail panel. The system creates a new encounter record linked to the appointment and opens the note editor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm encounter details">
    Review the pre-populated encounter details — visit type, rendering provider, facility, and date of service. Edit any fields if needed before proceeding.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select a note type">
    Choose a note type from the **Note Type** dropdown: **SOAP Note**, **Progress Note**, **H&P**, or any custom template your practice has configured. The editor loads the corresponding structure automatically.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document the encounter">
    Complete each section of the note. Use the AI-assisted documentation tools, smart text macros, or free-text entry as appropriate for your workflow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign or save as draft">
    When documentation is complete, click **Sign Note** to finalize, or **Save Draft** to return to the note later. Unsigned notes appear in your **Inbox** under **Unsigned Notes**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  You can also start an encounter from the **Patient Chart** by navigating to the **Encounters** tab and clicking **New Encounter**. This is useful for documenting telephone encounters, portal messages, or other non-appointment-based visits.
</Tip>

## Note Types

The EHR supports several structured note formats designed for different clinical contexts. Each note type provides a standardized template while remaining fully customizable.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="SOAP Note">
    The SOAP note is the standard format for outpatient office visits. It organizes clinical thinking into four discrete sections:

    | Section | Purpose |
    |---|---|
    | **Subjective** | Chief complaint, history of present illness (HPI), review of systems (ROS), and the patient's own reported symptoms |
    | **Objective** | Vital signs, physical exam findings, and relevant diagnostic data reviewed during the visit |
    | **Assessment** | Clinical diagnoses or problems addressed during the visit, linked to ICD-10 codes for billing |
    | **Plan** | Treatment decisions, prescriptions ordered, labs or imaging ordered, referrals placed, and follow-up instructions |

    **Best used for:** Established patient office visits, acute care visits, urgent care encounters, and telehealth visits.

    Each SOAP section supports inline order entry — you can place prescriptions, labs, or referrals directly from within the **Plan** section without leaving the note editor.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Progress Note">
    A Progress Note is a streamlined format designed for follow-up visits where a full SOAP structure may be unnecessary. It focuses on interval changes, ongoing management, and updated care plans.

    | Section | Purpose |
    |---|---|
    | **Interval History** | Changes in symptoms, adherence to treatment, and any new concerns since the last visit |
    | **Exam** | Focused physical exam findings relevant to the active problems |
    | **Problems Addressed** | List of conditions managed during this visit with their current status |
    | **Plan Updates** | Medication changes, new orders, referrals, and patient instructions |

    **Best used for:** Chronic disease management visits, post-procedure follow-ups, and care management encounters.

    Progress Notes automatically pull forward active medications and problem list entries from the patient's chart, reducing redundant data entry.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="History & Physical (H&P)">
    The History & Physical is a comprehensive note format used for new patients or hospital admissions. It captures a complete clinical picture from scratch.

    | Section | Purpose |
    |---|---|
    | **Chief Complaint** | The primary reason for the visit in the patient's own words |
    | **History of Present Illness** | Detailed narrative of the current problem |
    | **Past Medical History** | Prior diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgical history |
    | **Family History** | Hereditary conditions and family health background |
    | **Social History** | Lifestyle factors including tobacco, alcohol, occupation, and housing |
    | **Review of Systems** | Systematic inquiry across organ systems |
    | **Physical Examination** | Complete multi-system exam findings |
    | **Assessment & Plan** | Diagnoses and comprehensive care plan |

    **Best used for:** New patient visits, annual wellness exams, pre-operative evaluations, and hospital admissions.

    The H&P imports existing data from the patient's chart — prior diagnoses, medications, and allergies — so you only need to verify and update, not re-enter from scratch.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Using AI-Assisted Documentation

The AI documentation assistant reduces the time you spend typing by generating draft note content from ambient audio or structured prompts. You remain in full control — the AI drafts, and you review, edit, and sign.

<Note>
  AI-assisted documentation is an optional add-on feature. Contact your account administrator to enable it for your practice. All AI-generated content is clearly labeled as a draft until a licensed provider reviews and signs the note.
</Note>

**Ambient listening mode** captures the provider-patient conversation and populates the relevant note sections automatically:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enable ambient mode">
    Open the note editor and click the **microphone icon** in the toolbar to activate ambient listening. Grant microphone permissions if prompted by your browser.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Conduct the visit naturally">
    Speak with your patient as you normally would. The AI transcribes and structures the conversation in real time. A live transcript appears in the sidebar panel so you can monitor accuracy.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop recording">
    Click the **microphone icon** again to end the session. The AI processes the transcript and populates the note sections within a few seconds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and edit the draft">
    Carefully read every AI-generated section. Edit, expand, or remove content as needed to ensure the note accurately reflects the clinical encounter. Never sign an AI-generated draft without a thorough review.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can also use **AI Smart Prompts** to generate specific sections. Type `/ai` in any text field to open the prompt box, then describe what you want — for example, "Write an HPI for a 45-year-old with worsening knee pain for 3 weeks."

## Signing and Co-signing Notes

Signing a clinical note constitutes a legal attestation that the documented information is accurate and complete to the best of your professional knowledge.

<Warning>
  **Signing a note is a legally binding action.** Once signed, the note becomes part of the official medical record. You cannot delete a signed note — you can only amend it by adding an addendum. Ensure every note section is accurate and complete before clicking **Sign Note**. If a note was created by a trainee or supervised provider, the supervising provider must co-sign before the note is considered finalized for billing purposes.
</Warning>

### Signing your own note

1. Open the note from the **Encounters** tab in the patient chart or from **Inbox → Unsigned Notes**.
2. Review every section carefully.
3. Click **Sign Note**. The system records your digital signature, the date and time, and your provider credentials.

### Co-signing a trainee's note

Supervising providers receive a co-sign request in their **Inbox** when a supervised provider — such as a resident, medical student, or nurse practitioner under supervision — completes a note.

1. Open the co-sign request from your **Inbox**.
2. Review the note in full. You may edit any section before co-signing.
3. Click **Co-sign Note**. Your attestation is added below the trainee's signature with a timestamp.

<Tip>
  Configure automatic co-sign routing in **Settings → Provider Supervision Rules** so that notes from supervised providers are always routed to the correct supervising physician without manual assignment.
</Tip>

## Addenda

When you need to correct or supplement a signed note, add an addendum rather than attempting to alter the original entry. Addenda preserve the integrity of the medical record while allowing you to provide necessary clarifications.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the signed note">
    Navigate to the patient's chart, go to the **Encounters** tab, and click the signed note you need to amend.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click 'Add Addendum'">
    Scroll to the bottom of the signed note and click the **Add Addendum** button. A new text field opens below the original note content.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the addendum">
    Enter your addendum text. Be specific about what you are clarifying, correcting, or supplementing. Reference the original note date and the reason for the addendum.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign the addendum">
    Click **Sign Addendum**. The addendum is appended to the note with your name, credentials, and the date and time of the addendum — clearly distinguished from the original signed content.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  Addenda are visible to all providers with access to the patient's chart and are included in any chart export or release of records request. They cannot be deleted after signing.
</Info>
